---
name: TERAS capability worldview
description: Strategic worldview connecting TERAS Academy to TERAS Institute's broader systems and capability agenda.
---

TERAS should be understood through capability formation rather than through projects, assets, or investment volume alone. Natural resources and capital are starting conditions; the development outcome is the system's ability to create the next value, learn, coordinate, and adapt.

**Why:** TERAS working papers frame processing as different from capability, investment attraction as different from capability formation, and static competitiveness as different from adaptive competitiveness. Development should begin by understanding the characteristics of the capital or goods being managed, then designing institutions suited to those characteristics.

**How to apply:** Use the recurring logic Resource → Processing → Capability → Ecosystem → Learning → Adaptation. Evaluate energy, infrastructure, supply chains, skills, institutions, spatial pressures, ecology, knowledge, and future room for choice as connected systems rather than isolated indicators.

TERAS positions itself as a knowledge integrator: Evidence → Interpretation → System Understanding → Decision. This does not mean TERAS claims all knowledge; it connects fragmented knowledge so assumptions, trade-offs, trajectories, and feedback become usable in decisions.

**Why:** Investment and industrial activity can raise output without proportionate gains in domestic technology, supplier depth, quality employment, institutional capacity, or resilience.

**How to apply:** Treat TERAS Academy as one operational instrument for human and institutional capability development. Do not force every TERAS Institute idea into the Academy product; include it only when it improves a concrete learning, evidence, readiness, or decision-support outcome.
